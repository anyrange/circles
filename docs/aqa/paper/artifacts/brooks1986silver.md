# Brooks (1986) -- No Silver Bullet: Essence and Accident in Software Engineering

**File:** `sources/Brooks_1986_-_No_Silver_Bullet.pdf`
**Venue:** University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill Technical Report, 1986
**BibTeX key:** `brooks1986silver`

## What It Says

- Software difficulty is driven by both essential complexity and accidental complexity.
- No single technology or management technique can deliver an order-of-magnitude improvement by itself.
- Accidental difficulties can still be reduced, even if they do not remove the essential complexity of software.
- Complexity creates communication, reliability, and change-management problems in large systems.

## Key Quotes

> "There is no single development, in either technology or management technique" (p. 1)

> "Most of the big past gains in software productivity have come from removing artificial barriers" (p. 1)

## How to Use in Paper

- **Related Work:** Frame toolchain fragmentation as an accidental source of complexity rather than the whole software problem.
- **Discussion:** Temper claims about unification by showing that it removes one class of avoidable complexity, not all engineering difficulty.
